About

A steady home base

A place to gather, to return to, and to reconnect — held quietly on a hill above the Rogue River Valley.

Our story

Open since the mid-1980s

St Rita's Retreat opened in the mid-1980s on a rise of oak-covered hillside outside Gold Hill, Oregon. It was built for a single purpose, and that purpose has not changed: to give people a quiet place to step out of ordinary time for a few days.

Since then the hill has held parish weekends and silent retreats, recovery fellowships and ministry gatherings, and a great many people who simply needed somewhere to be alone with their thoughts. Many of the groups who come have been coming for decades.

The buildings have been cared for and added to over the years — new stations along the hillside, tended gardens, repaired paths — but the scale has stayed deliberately small.

What we offer

Simple comfort, without distraction

Room to be quiet

No televisions in the rooms, no crowded schedule, no reason to hurry. The house is kept quiet by common agreement, and guests are trusted to keep it that way for one another.

Space that fits the day

A chapel for prayer, a library for reading, a lounge for conversation, and meeting rooms for groups that need them — so a retreat can gather and disperse as it needs to.

Supportive hosting

Clean rooms, meals arranged in advance, and someone on hand when a question comes up. Our aim is that the practical details stay out of your way entirely.

The setting

Southern Oregon, in every direction

The valley below is known for its rivers, its mountains, and its long dry summers. The Rogue runs a few minutes from the gate. Medford, Jacksonville, and Ashland are all within easy reach, and the Oregon Shakespeare Festival draws visitors from across the country each season.

Some guests plan their retreat around the region and use the hill as a base — a morning on the water, an afternoon in a historic downtown, an evening back here where it is quiet. Others come up the drive and stay put until it is time to leave.

We have no opinion about which is better. The place works either way.
