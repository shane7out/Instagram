How an accessibility review works

  1. We test your key pages against the WCAG accessibility guidelines, with automated tools and by hand.
  2. We list the problems in plain language, such as images without descriptions, low contrast and forms without labels.
  3. We fix them, including keyboard use and screen reader behavior.
  4. We recheck and keep new pages from slipping back.

Before it goes live

Automated tools catch only part of the problems, so a real review also tests with a keyboard and a screen reader. Accessibility work reduces risk and makes a site usable for more people, but nothing here is legal advice and no one can promise immunity from a claim.

Common questions

Does this make my site ADA compliant?

It aims to meet the WCAG guidelines that courts and guidance commonly refer to. Compliance is a legal question, so we do not promise a legal result.

Why test by hand?

Tools miss many issues, such as confusing keyboard order or unclear labels.
