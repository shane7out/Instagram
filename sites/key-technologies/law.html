The Firm Problem

The firm that calls back first usually signs the client.

A potential client who doesn't hear back within the day typically calls the next firm on the list. Generic legal CRMs track matters once they're open — they don't catch and route the first call, qualify it, or follow up automatically before the case ever becomes a file. That gap is where real retainers get lost.

FAQ

Questions, answered

Do you work with solo practitioners or larger firms?

Both. The system gets sized to your firm — a solo practitioner needs different automation than a 20-attorney firm, and the build reflects that.

Can this integrate with Clio, MyCase, or other practice management software?

Depends on the tool — some integrate directly, others get replaced if that's genuinely the better fit. That gets figured out in the first conversation, not assumed beforehand.

How is this different from a generic legal CRM?

Generic legal CRMs are built to be generic enough for every firm in the country. This gets built specifically around how your firm actually takes in a client — your intake process, your follow-up cadence, your existing tools — not a one-size-fits-all platform you adapt yourself to.

Who controls our case data?

Your case data, your account and your systems stay in your name — Key Technologies doesn't host or own any of it. Doing the actual work does mean trusting Key Technologies with real login access, the same as hiring any contractor. In return, that work is backed by a team with 35+ years of combined security, networking and technology experience, doing everything reasonable to keep things secure. In decades of having access to client systems and databases, there's never been a client compromised.
