How much does an AI receptionist cost? What actually sets the price

Key Technologies · Published November 3, 2026

Short answer: there is no single price, and be wary of any vendor who quotes one without asking questions. The cost depends mostly on how many calls you get, what the assistant is allowed to do, what it connects to, and who looks after it. This guide shows you what to ask so you can compare quotes fairly. We do not publish a price list here, because the right number depends on those answers.

What an AI receptionist does

An AI receptionist answers your business phone, says that it is an AI, answers common questions from information you approved (hours, services, location), collects the caller’s details, and can propose an appointment. Anything urgent, unclear or sensitive goes to a real person. You can see this in our AI phone receptionist demo, which uses a made-up business and is not a real phone line.

What drives the price

  1. Call volume. More calls and longer calls mean more usage. Ask whether you pay per minute, per call or per month.
  2. What it is allowed to do. Answering common questions is simple. Booking into a calendar, texting confirmations or taking payments each add setup and risk.
  3. Connections to your tools. Calendar, customer list (CRM), email and text messaging each need to be connected and kept working.
  4. Phone line setup. Forwarding your existing number, getting a new number or moving a number between providers can each carry a one-time or monthly cost.
  5. Human handoff and after-hours rules. Who gets the urgent call at 9 p.m.? Setting that up well takes thought.
  6. Languages and voices. Extra languages or a custom voice can cost more.
  7. Rules you must follow. Telling callers it is an AI and getting consent to record calls vary by state. Getting this right takes setup time.
  8. Who looks after it. Someone has to watch the calls, fix wrong answers and update information when your hours or prices change. Check whether that is included.

How vendors usually charge

  • A monthly plan with a set number of calls or minutes included.
  • Pay per minute or per call, which can be cheap when quiet and surprising when busy.
  • A one-time setup fee plus a monthly service fee.
  • Per booked appointment or per lead, which sounds fair but needs a clear definition of what counts.

Costs people miss

  • Overage charges when you go past included calls or minutes.
  • Extra fees for each connected tool or each extra number.
  • Charges for changes after setup (new hours, new services).
  • Minimum contract lengths and cancellation terms.
  • Your own time to review calls and correct the assistant at the start.

Questions to ask any vendor

  1. What is included in the monthly price, and what counts as extra?
  2. What happens when I go over my calls or minutes?
  3. Will it tell callers it is an AI, and how are call recordings and consent handled?
  4. What will it never answer on its own, and where do those calls go?
  5. Which of my tools does it connect to, and who maintains those connections?
  6. Who fixes a wrong answer, and how fast?
  7. Can I leave, and do I keep my phone number and my data?
  8. Can I hear real sample calls, not only a recorded demo?

How to estimate what it is worth to you

Use your own numbers, not a vendor’s. For a week, count: (1) the calls you miss or send to voicemail, (2) roughly how many of those would have become a paying customer, and (3) what an average customer is worth. Multiply them together. That is the order of magnitude of what missed calls cost you, and it tells you how much an AI receptionist could be worth. If the figure is small, a simpler fix such as a better voicemail message may be enough, and we will tell you so.

Common questions

Is an AI receptionist cheaper than a human receptionist?

It depends on your call volume and what you need the calls to do. An AI receptionist is often used for after-hours calls and overflow, and it does not replace a person who greets customers in your shop. Compare it to the cost of the calls you miss today, not only to a salary.

Why do vendors not publish one price?

Because the work differs a lot from business to business: call volume, connected tools, what the assistant is allowed to do and who maintains it. Be careful with any quote given without asking about those.

Do I need to tell callers it is an AI?

Plan to. Rules on AI disclosure and on recording calls differ by state, so decide your wording and consent steps before going live and ask a lawyer about your situation.
