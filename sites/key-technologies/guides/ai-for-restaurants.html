AI for restaurants: 6 practical ways to save time

Key Technologies · Published November 3, 2026

Short answer: the best uses of AI in a restaurant are the repetitive, rules-based jobs: answering the same phone questions, sending reminders, drafting replies and posts. A person should stay in charge of anything involving food safety, money, complaints and guests who need real hospitality. Start with one job, not six.

Restaurant owners are busy and the phone does not stop. Here are six jobs where AI can help today. Each one has a demo on this site with made-up data, so you can see how it behaves before you think about your own business.

1. Answering the phone

What it does: picks up when your team is busy or closed, says it is an AI, answers questions about hours, location, parking and menu, takes a message and passes urgent calls to a person.

What stays human: complaints, large events, anything about allergies or safety, and any call the assistant is unsure about.

Try the AI phone receptionist demo

2. Booking and reminders

What it does: lets a guest pick a time, sends a confirmation, and sends reminders so fewer people forget. It can also free up a table when someone cancels.

What stays human: special requests and your rules for large parties. If you already use a reservation platform, AI works best beside it rather than replacing it.

Try the booking and reminders demo

3. Replying to reviews

What it does: collects new reviews in one place and drafts a polite reply in your voice, so you answer every review quickly.

What stays human: you approve every reply, and negative reviews go to you first. It should never write fake reviews or offer rewards for good ones, which the review sites forbid.

Try the review management demo

4. Catering and event email

What it does: sorts incoming email into catering requests, vendor questions and everything else, and drafts a first reply with your menu and policies.

What stays human: quotes, contracts and refunds. A person reads and approves each reply before it is sent.

Try the AI email assistant demo

5. Social media posts

What it does: drafts captions and post ideas around your dishes, specials and events in your tone, on a weekly calendar.

What stays human: your real photos and your approval. Guests can tell generic content from the real thing, so use AI for the first draft and your own food for the picture.

Try the content creator demo · Try the social media demo

6. A menu and policy assistant

What it does: answers staff and guests from your own documents: menu, hours, gift card rules, parking, event packages. If the answer is not in the documents, it says so.

What stays human: keep the source documents current, and have a person handle anything about allergens that is not clearly written down.

Try the knowledge assistant demo

Where not to use AI

  • Allergy and food-safety answers that are not in your approved information. Send them to a person.
  • Refunds, comps and disputes. A person decides.
  • Fake reviews or fake testimonials. Never.
  • Anything you would not be comfortable explaining to the guest. If a guest would feel tricked, do not do it.

How to start

  1. Pick the one task that costs you the most time or the most missed business.
  2. Try its demo so you know what good looks like.
  3. Write down what must always go to a person.
  4. Start small, review the results every week for the first month, then decide on the next task.

Common questions

Will AI replace my staff?

No. The useful jobs are the repetitive ones: answering the same questions, sending reminders and drafting replies. Guests still want a person for a welcome, a problem or a special occasion.

Can AI answer questions about allergies?

Not on its own. Allergy and food-safety questions should come from your approved menu information, and anything uncertain or urgent should go straight to a person on your team.

Does AI work with OpenTable or my reservation system?

It depends on the system and what it allows. Many restaurants already use a reservation platform, and AI is often best placed beside it, handling phone questions and reminders. We would check your setup first.

Is this expensive to try?

Start small. Pick the one task that costs you the most time, such as missed phone calls or review replies, and try that first. Our demos are free to try and use made-up data.
