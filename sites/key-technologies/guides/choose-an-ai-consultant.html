How to choose an AI consultant: 10 questions to ask

Key Technologies · Published November 5, 2026

Short answer: choose the consultant who starts with your problem, shows you something real, says what AI will not do, and leaves you owning your accounts and data. The ten questions below help you check each of those. We are an AI consultancy ourselves, so we have written this to be useful whoever you hire.

The ten questions

  1. What problem do you think I should solve first? A good answer comes from questions about your business. A weak answer starts with a tool or a product name.
  2. Can I see something working? Look for a demo you can try or a live system they run themselves. Slides are not proof.
  3. What will the AI not do on its own? You want a clear line: what is automatic, what needs your approval, and what stays with a person.
  4. What happens when the AI is wrong? AI can be confidently wrong. Ask how mistakes are caught, logged and fixed.
  5. Who owns my accounts, domain, customer list and data? The answer should be you, in your name.
  6. What access do you need, and how is it kept safe? Real work needs login access. Ask about passwords, who has access, and what happens if something goes wrong.
  7. How will we know it is working? Agree on one or two simple measures before you start, such as missed calls or hours saved, and check them after.
  8. What will it cost, in writing? Ask for a written scope that says what is included, what is extra, and what ongoing costs there are.
  9. What rules apply to my business? Telling callers they are speaking to an AI, recording calls, privacy, and advertising rules differ by place and industry. A good consultant raises these, and you should check with a lawyer where it matters.
  10. Can I leave, and what do I keep? You should be able to take your systems, data and phone number with you.

Warning signs

  • Promises of guaranteed results, instant revenue, or replacing your whole team.
  • No written scope, or a price that changes without your request changing.
  • Pressure to decide on the first call.
  • Reluctance to show anything you can try yourself.
  • Asking to own or hold your accounts instead of using access you grant.
  • Skipping the limits: no word about mistakes, privacy or when a person steps in.

What a good first conversation looks like

It should feel like a diagnosis: questions about how your business runs today, where time or customers are lost, and what has been tried. You should leave knowing whether AI helps, where it helps first, and what a small first step looks like. Sometimes the honest answer is that a simpler fix works better.

Common questions

Do I need an AI consultant, or can I use a chatbot myself?

You can start on your own for drafting and brainstorming. A consultant helps when AI has to connect to your phones, customer list or website, when customers will see the output, or when security and accuracy matter.

What should I prepare before the first call?

Write down the one or two jobs that cost you the most time or customers, the tools you use today, and what a good result would look like. You do not need to know which AI tools you want.

Should I be worried if a consultant will not name a price on the first call?

No. A fair quote needs to know what you want built. Be careful if there is no written scope afterwards, or if the price keeps changing without a change in what you asked for.

Who should own the accounts and data?

You. Your accounts, domain, customer list and data should stay in your name. A good consultant needs login access to do the work, but should never become the owner.

How can I check a consultant is real?

Ask to see work you can try yourself, such as a demo or a system they run for their own business, and talk to them directly, not only to a sales form.
