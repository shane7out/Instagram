Weekly layout

Mix of video types

  • Short (under about a minute): about 6 of every 10. One idea, one tip.
  • How-to (2 to 5 minutes): about 2 of every 10. One task, step by step.
  • Demo walkthrough: about 1 of every 10. Screen-record a site demo (the data is made up).
  • Long-form (8 to 15 minutes): about 1 a week. The week's best Shorts in one deeper video.

Layout of a Short

0 to 3 secHook: the problem in one sentence ("Your phone rings during the dinner rush. Who answers?").
3 to 25 secThe one point, in plain words, shown on screen.
25 to 45 secShow it working in a demo (made-up data, labeled).
Last 5 secOne call to action: "Free call, link below" or "Try the demo, link below."

Honest first. Nobody can promise views or customers from posting a lot. What works is videos that are clearly useful to a business owner, posted steadily, with one clear next step. Volume only helps if each video is good, so the plan ramps up instead of starting at ten a day.

The goal

Business owners (restaurants, dental and medical offices, chiropractors, law firms) watch a short, useful video, click through to the site and book a free call.

Phases

Weeks 1 to 2: 3 a dayTwo Shorts and one how-to. Prove the routine and the quality. Set up each channel (name, banner, about text, playlists per theme).
Weeks 3 to 6: 5 a dayAdd a demo walkthrough and one more Short. Each Sunday, look at the numbers and make more of what people finish watching.
Week 7 on: up to 10 a dayOnly if every video still passes the checklist and people are watching. If quality slips, drop back to 5.

You can go faster. Just know the main risk of high volume is repeating yourself, which viewers and the platforms both notice.

How each video gets made and posted

  1. Idea from the Ideas tab, matching the day's theme.
  2. Script (AI can draft it): hook, one point, demo, call to action.
  3. Make it: your own voice or an AI voice, screen recordings of the demos, simple captions.
  4. Review: a person watches it start to finish and runs the checklist below.
  5. Post kit: tap Post kit on the video. It writes the caption for each platform with a tracked link, then opens your phone's share sheet so you can send the video to YouTube, Instagram, TikTok or Facebook.
  6. Tick the platform chips as each one goes live.

Post once, share to all four

  • On your phone (works today): the Post kit's Share button opens the phone's own share sheet. Pick the video, pick the app, paste the caption (it is already copied).
  • Scheduling tools: Meta Business Suite can schedule Instagram and Facebook posts together, YouTube Studio schedules YouTube, and TikTok has a web uploader. Several third-party tools publish to all four from one screen. Check each one's current limits and cost yourself before choosing.
  • Fully automatic (drop a video and it posts everywhere): possible, but it needs a private server and each platform's approval of an app (YouTube, Meta and TikTok each review these, and can say no or limit it to private posts until approved). That is weeks of work and your account logins. Worth it only once the routine is proven.

Things to know about the platforms

  • Repetitive, mass-produced videos can lose monetization or reach. Templates are fine. Each video still needs its own useful point. Read each platform's current rules before ramping up.
  • Label realistic AI-made content. YouTube, Instagram and TikTok each have a setting for it. Use it, and say so in the description.
  • Links: Instagram and TikTok captions do not make links clickable. Use the link in your bio. YouTube and Facebook descriptions can hold clickable links.
  • New channels start slow. Expect weeks before the numbers mean much. Judge by watch time and clicks, not views alone.

What to look at each Sunday

  • Average view duration and retention: do people stay past the first few seconds?
  • Clicks to the site: the Visitors report, Sources tab, shows youtube, instagram, facebook or tiktok for tracked links.
  • Forms sent (Actions tab). That is the number that matters most.
  • Make more of the top 3 videos' themes. Drop what nobody finishes.

Ground rules

  • No made-up numbers, clients, reviews or rankings. Demos say their data is made up.
  • No prices and no promises of results.
  • Do not clone anyone's face or voice without their permission. Your own, with your OK, is fine.
  • Do not put an email address in a description or caption. Send people to the site and the free-call page.
  • Do not say a system "connects to" a specific tool unless it really does.
