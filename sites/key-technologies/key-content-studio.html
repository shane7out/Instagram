Mix of video types

  • Short (under about a minute): about 6 of every 10. One idea, one tip.
  • How-to (2 to 5 minutes): about 2 of every 10. Walk through one task step by step.
  • Demo walkthrough: about 1 of every 10. Screen-record a demo from the site with the made-up data.
  • Long-form (8 to 15 minutes): about 1 a week. Combine the week's best Shorts into one deeper video.

Layout of a Short

0 to 3 secHook: the problem in one sentence ("Your phone rings during the dinner rush. Who answers?").
3 to 25 secThe one point. Plain words. Show it on screen.
25 to 45 secShow it working in a demo (made-up data, labeled).
Last 5 secOne call to action: "Free call, link below" or "Try the demo, link below."

Honest first. Nobody can promise views or customers from posting a lot. What works is videos that are clearly useful to a business owner, posted steadily, with one clear next step. Volume only helps if each video is good. So the plan ramps up instead of starting at ten a day.

The goal

Business owners (restaurants, dental and medical offices, chiropractors, law firms) watch a short, useful video, then click through to the site and book a free call.

Phases

  1. Weeks 1 to 2: 3 a day. Two Shorts and one how-to. Goal: prove the routine and the quality, and set up the channel (name, banner, about text, playlists per theme).
  2. Weeks 3 to 6: 5 a day. Add a demo walkthrough and one more Short. Look at the numbers every Sunday and make more of whatever people finish watching.
  3. Week 7 on: up to 10 a day, only if every video still passes the checklist below and the numbers show people watching. If quality slips, drop back to 5.

You can go faster. Just know the main risk of high volume is repeating yourself, which viewers and YouTube both notice.

How each video gets made

  1. Idea from the Ideas tab, matching the day's theme.
  2. Script (AI can draft it): hook, one point, demo, call to action.
  3. Make it: your own voice or an AI voice, screen recordings of the demos, simple captions.
  4. Review: a person watches it start to finish and runs the checklist.
  5. Schedule or post with the tracked link in the description (Links tab).
  6. Mark it Posted here.

Things to know about YouTube

  • Repetitive, mass-produced videos can lose monetization or reach. Templates are fine. Each video still needs its own useful point. Read YouTube's current policy on "inauthentic content" before ramping up, because the rules change.
  • Label realistic AI-made content. YouTube asks you to say when realistic video or audio is altered or synthetic. Use the setting in YouTube Studio's upload form, and say so in the description.
  • Automated uploading is limited. Uploading through YouTube's developer connection has a daily cap by default and an approval step. Uploading by hand or with YouTube's own scheduler has no such problem. Check before building automation on it.
  • New channels start slow. Expect weeks before the numbers mean much. Judge by watch time and clicks, not by views alone.

What to watch each Sunday

  • Average view duration and retention (YouTube Studio): do people stay past the first few seconds?
  • Which videos get clicks to the site: the Visitors report, Sources tab, will show "youtube" for tracked links.
  • Forms sent (Actions tab). That is the number that matters most.
  • Make more of the top 3 videos' themes. Drop what nobody finishes.

Ground rules for this channel

  • No made-up numbers, clients, reviews or rankings. Demos say their data is made up.
  • No prices and no promises of results.
  • Do not clone anyone's face or voice without their permission. Your own, with your OK, is fine.
  • Do not put an email address in descriptions. Send people to the site and the free-call page.
  • Do not claim a system "connects to" a specific tool unless it really does.
