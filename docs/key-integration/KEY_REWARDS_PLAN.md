# Key Rewards: concept plan (draft for Shane and, later, an attorney)

Status: IDEA ONLY. Nothing here is built or live. Written 2026-11-06 from Shane's description. Not legal advice.

## The idea in Shane's words
Key Technologies is the umbrella. It issues "Key Rewards" and works with every restaurant Shane works with, anywhere in the world.
Each restaurant makes a deal with Shane and covers the cost of the free food itself; it knows this is Shane's own rewards system.
Event example: 200 people are at a restaurant, the restaurant puts up 2,000 rewards, and the 2,000 are split equally among the people who are really there (10 each). Keep it simple.

## How the event split would work (simplest safe version)
1. The restaurant and Shane agree the pool in advance (for example 2,000 rewards), the date and time window, and a cap. The restaurant knows its maximum cost.
2. At the restaurant, staff show a QR code (or a short code that changes each event). Each member scans once, inside the window.
3. When the window closes, the pool is divided equally among the verified scans (2,000 / 200 = 10 each). Round down; any leftover stays in the pool.
4. Members see the rewards in their account. Nothing is paid in cash.
5. If very few people scan, the split per person would be large. Decide a per-person maximum (for example never more than a set number) so a quiet night cannot hand one person the whole pool.

## Using the rewards
- Rewards can only be used for free food offers at participating restaurants. No cash value, cannot be sold or exchanged, never refunded once used (same wording as the live program).
- Every offer is at least 15 rewards (existing rule). A 10-reward event share cannot be used on its own; the member collects more, which brings them back.
- The restaurant that honors an offer covers it. Staff confirm each redemption with a code.

## Deal with each restaurant (a short written agreement)
- Who honors offers, which offers, and any blackout days.
- The pool size and cap for each event; the restaurant knows its maximum exposure.
- How redemption is confirmed and how long rewards stay usable at that restaurant.
- What happens if the restaurant leaves the program (rewards already issued by it, and how members are told).
- That Key Technologies runs the system and the restaurant is not selling or guaranteeing anything beyond its own offers.

## Worldwide (why it needs care)
- Loyalty, promotion and consumer-protection rules differ by country and state; so do data-protection rules for check-in and location data (for example GDPR in Europe).
- "1 reward = $1" is a dollar value. Other countries mean other currencies, taxes and wording.
- Suggestion: build it so no rule is tied to one place, launch where the attorney has cleared it (Las Vegas first), and add countries one at a time.

## Questions for an attorney
1. Which legal entity issues the rewards (Key Technologies, LVR, or another)? Does the umbrella structure change the Terms?
2. Does an equal split by verified check-in need any special terms or disclosures?
3. Check-in records and location: what must the Privacy Policy say?
4. Is the restaurant deal best as a short agreement, and what must it include?
5. Does using the name "Key Rewards" need a trademark search first? (The current program name is "LVR Rewards".)
6. What must change before the program is offered outside the United States?

## Not decided yet
Name of the live program (LVR Rewards vs Key Rewards); per-person cap; how staff confirm headcount; which countries and when.

## Update 2026-11-07: no food, no expiry

Still true: no cash value, cannot be sold or exchanged for cash, minimum use 15, each venue agrees its pool in advance.
